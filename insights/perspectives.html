Point of View

How We Think About the Federal Technology Landscape

These perspectives reflect how Ravonics and INSTAR Lab think about the key technology and policy trends shaping federal IT modernization. They are the product of our founder's 30+ years in AI and computer science and our affiliated research bench — not sanitized marketing language. Full white papers on each topic are forthcoming.

Compliance & Acquisition

Policy-as-Code Is the Only Scalable Answer to Federal Compliance Debt

Manual compliance assessment is the hidden tax on every federal software program. Point-in-time audits produce a snapshot that is stale by the time it is reviewed. Declarative policy frameworks that evaluate compliance at every pipeline gate — and produce signed, timestamped evidence automatically — are the only path to sustainable cATO at program scale.

Software Supply Chain

SBOM Is Not Optional — It Is the Foundation of Federal Software Trust

Executive Order 14028 made SBOM a federal requirement for critical software. But an SBOM without attestation and VEX is an inventory without verification. The full triad — Software Bill of Materials, artifact attestation, and Vulnerability Exploitability eXchange — is what gives a procurement officer actual assurance about what they are authorizing to run in a federal system.

Agentic AI

Agentic Delivery Changes What "Program" Means for Federal Software

When an autonomous agent can translate a capability requirement into architecture, code, tests, and a deployment artifact — with every decision logged and auditable — the bottleneck in a federal software program shifts from development to governance. That is a better problem to have. It is also a change that most acquisition frameworks are not yet designed for.

Post-Quantum

The Quantum Migration Window Is Shorter Than Most Programs Realize

NIST finalized FIPS 203, 204, and 205 in 2024. Federal systems handling sensitive data for 10+ years need to begin cryptographic agility planning now, not when a cryptographically relevant quantum computer materializes. The harvest-now-decrypt-later threat is real, and systems that rely on RSA or ECC for data confidentiality today are already exposed to that risk for any traffic being archived by an adversary.

Zero Trust

Zero Trust Is an Architecture, Not a Product

Federal mandates for zero trust (DoD ZT Strategy, OMB M-22-09) have created a market of vendors claiming "zero trust" as a product feature. The reality is that zero trust is an architectural posture requiring identity-centric access control, continuous authorization, micro-segmentation, and encrypted communications enforced at every layer. Immutable infrastructure and GitOps-driven deployment are among the most effective enablers — because they eliminate the configuration drift that undermines perimeter assumptions in the first place.

Small Business

HUBZone Is a Policy Tool, Not a Marketing Claim — Use It That Way

HUBZone set-asides exist to direct federal contracting dollars to historically underutilized business zones and the communities within them. Ravenswood, WV is one of those zones. We are building here because it is where our president is, and because the HUBZone designation — once our application is awarded — gives federal procurement officers a legitimate set-aside pathway while directing economic activity to a community that can use it. That alignment of program intent and business reality is rare, and worth stating plainly.
