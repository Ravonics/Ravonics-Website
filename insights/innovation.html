Research Partner

INSTAR Lab Inc. — Our R&D and Research Arm

Ravonics partners with INSTAR Lab Inc., a 501(c)(3) nonprofit research institute chaired by Ravonics founder Sean Hackney. INSTAR Lab is our SBIR/STTR research partner and applied physics research bench. The researchers affiliated with INSTAR Lab are independent; they are not Ravonics direct staff.

Applied Physics

Condensed-Matter and Materials Science Research

INSTAR Lab researchers specialize in material characterization and growth using thermal evaporation, sputtering, and small-angle X-ray scattering, with a focus on chalcogenide-based memory devices and high-energy particle-radiation effects. This research has direct relevance to radiation-hardened computing for space and defense environments.

Conducted by Dr. Spoogmay Khan, Research Physicist, INSTAR Lab. Ph.D. Physics & Astronomy, Ohio University (2026). Invited speaker, Materials Science & Technology conferences (2022–2024).

AI & Compliance Research

Applied Data Science, AI-ML Engineering, and CMMC/PQC Readiness

INSTAR Lab's AI research bench works at the intersection of physics-informed simulation, applied machine learning, and federal compliance frameworks. Areas of active work include CMMC/SOC compliance modeling and post-quantum cryptography readiness aligned to NIST FIPS 203/204/205 (ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA).

Led by Suzanne Conejos, Research Scientist (AI & Physics), INSTAR Lab. M.S. Physics, Ohio University. Currently serving as AI Tech Lead on a 10-engineer team. APS and ACM member. Peer-reviewed publications.

Agentic AI

Agentic Software Delivery Research

Ravonics is building toward a multi-tier autonomous agent orchestration paradigm for software delivery — from requirement ingestion through architecture, code, and deployment — with full decision audit trails and human-in-the-loop controls at each stage. This is our genuine technical differentiator for federal software programs.

Forthcoming white paper: "Hierarchical Agentic Software Delivery: From Requirement to Deployed Capability."

Post-Quantum Readiness

Post-Quantum Cryptography Readiness Framework

As NIST finalizes FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), 204 (ML-DSA), and 205 (SLH-DSA), federal systems face an active migration requirement. Our research develops a readiness framework covering dependency mapping, cryptographic agility, and migration sequencing for federal IT modernization programs.

Forthcoming white paper: "Post-Quantum Readiness for Federal Systems (FIPS 203/204/205)."

Supply Chain Security

Software Supply Chain Integrity: SBOM, Attestation, and VEX

Federal software security increasingly centers on provenance — knowing what is in a system, who built it, and whether known vulnerabilities are exploitable. Our research examines SBOM, artifact attestation, and Vulnerability Exploitability eXchange (VEX) as the interoperable standards underpinning federal provenance requirements.

Forthcoming white paper: "Defending the Software Supply Chain: SBOM, Attestation & VEX."

Multi-Domain Awareness

Open-Source Data Fusion for Multi-Domain Situational Awareness

Fusing publicly available maritime (AIS), airspace (ADS-B), satellite, weather, and environmental data streams into a coherent multi-domain operational picture using open standards. Research examines methodology, data quality, latency, and the role of AI-driven object detection in completing the picture.

Forthcoming white paper: "Multi-Domain Situational Awareness Through Open-Source Data Fusion."
