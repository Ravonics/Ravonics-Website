Policy-as-Code for Federal Software Procurement

  • Insights
  • Technical Publications
  • Policy-as-Code
FORTHCOMING This white paper is in preparation. No publication date, DOI, or journal acceptance is claimed. The abstract below is a preview of the planned work.

Ravonics LLC — Technical White Paper Series

Abstract

Federal software acquisition has historically treated compliance as a point-in-time checkpoint — an audit performed after a system is built, not a constraint woven into how it is built. This paper examines how declarative policy frameworks can change that by making compliance machine-enforceable throughout the acquisition and delivery lifecycle.

Policy-as-code applies the same infrastructure-as-code principle to compliance: policies are expressed as versioned, testable artifacts that gates in a software supply chain can evaluate automatically. When a build pipeline reaches a compliance gate, it evaluates the policy and either advances or halts — with a machine-readable evidence record documenting the evaluation. That record feeds directly into the Authorization to Operate (ATO) package.

Key Areas of Examination

  1. The Cost of Late Compliance Discovery

    Federal programs routinely encounter compliance findings late in the delivery cycle, when remediation is most expensive and schedule impact is largest. This section examines the structural causes — siloed compliance teams, point-in-time assessment models, and manual evidence collection — and quantifies the upstream opportunity of earlier enforcement.

  2. Declarative Policy Frameworks for Federal Contexts

    Open policy agent ecosystems and related declarative tools allow security and compliance teams to express NIST 800-53 controls, CMMC practices, and agency-specific requirements as versioned policy documents. This section surveys applicable frameworks and their fitness for federal security envelopes.

  3. Integration with the Software Supply Chain

    Policy gates inserted at key stages — source commit, container build, artifact signing, and deployment — create a continuous compliance signal. Each gate produces a signed, timestamped evidence artifact. Aggregated, these artifacts constitute a machine-generated audit package aligned to control families in NIST 800-53 Rev 5.

  4. ATO Timeline Implications

    Continuous Authorization to Operate (cATO) frameworks endorsed by DoD CDAO and NSA require an ongoing, automated evidence posture rather than periodic manual assessments. Policy-as-code is the enabling mechanism: it converts a compliance posture from a snapshot to a continuous stream that authorizing officials can observe in near-real time.

  5. Procurement Integration — Embedding Policy Requirements in Acquisition Language

    This section proposes a model for embedding policy-as-code requirements in federal solicitations — specifying what policy evidence a vendor must produce, in what format, at which pipeline stages — so that compliance rigor becomes a contractual deliverable rather than an inspection after the fact.
