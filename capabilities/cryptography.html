Post-Quantum Cryptography & Blockchain for Defense Systems

We design and engineer post-quantum cryptographic systems and distributed ledger technologies for federal missions. Our post-quantum encryption is built to protect against emerging quantum threats while blockchain provides tamper-evident audit trails for supply chain integrity. Our INSTAR Lab research bench includes active work in post-quantum cryptography readiness aligned to NIST FIPS 203, 204, and 205. From hardware security modules to zero-knowledge proofs, we build cryptographic architectures with mathematically grounded protections for sensitive federal data.

Post-quantum cryptographic architectures designed to protect sensitive data as quantum computing capabilities mature.

Key Capabilities

Post-Quantum Cryptography

NIST-compliant algorithms including lattice-based and hash-based cryptography protect against future quantum computing attacks, ensuring sensitive data remains protected as quantum capabilities mature.

Blockchain & Distributed Ledger

Tamper-proof audit trails and smart contracts for supply chain integrity, ammunition tracking, and multi-party workflows with cryptographically verified provenance and immutable records.

Hardware Security Modules (HSMs)

Tamper-resistant cryptographic processors protect key material with physical safeguards; architectures are designed to align with FIPS 140-3 validation requirements for federal security programs.

Zero-Knowledge Proofs

Cryptographic protocols enabling verification without revealing underlying data, critical for cross-domain information sharing and privacy-preserving authentication in coalition environments.

Cryptographic Key Management

Enterprise key lifecycle management with automated rotation, secure distribution, and emergency revocation across distributed systems ensuring continuous cryptographic operations at scale.

Side-Channel Attack Prevention

Countermeasures against power analysis, timing attacks, and electromagnetic emanations protecting cryptographic implementations from sophisticated hardware exploitation and reverse engineering.
