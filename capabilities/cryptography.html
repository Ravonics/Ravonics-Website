Post-Quantum Cryptography & Cryptographic Agility for Federal Systems

Ravonics provides post-quantum cryptography services designed to help federal programs get ahead of the quantum threat before it materializes. Our expertise spans algorithm selection and implementation aligned to NIST FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA), and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA); cryptographic agility architecture that decouples algorithm choice from application logic; and secure key lifecycle management. Combined with our zero-trust infrastructure practice, we help agencies build systems where cryptography is a living, maintainable layer — not a brittle point of failure. See our forthcoming white paper "Post-Quantum Readiness for Federal Systems (FIPS 203/204/205)" (Forthcoming) for our structured migration framework.

Post-quantum cryptography and cryptographic agility services that protect federal systems against current and future threats.

Key Capabilities

NIST PQC Algorithm Alignment

We provide implementation guidance and architecture review aligned to the three finalized NIST post-quantum standards: ML-KEM (FIPS 203) for key encapsulation, ML-DSA (FIPS 204) for digital signatures, and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) for stateless hash-based signatures.

Cryptographic Agility Architecture

Systems designed with cryptographic agility abstract algorithm selection behind stable interfaces, so future algorithm transitions — whether driven by new threats or updated standards — require configuration changes rather than architectural rework.

Secure Key Lifecycle Management

Key generation, storage, rotation, and revocation are managed through automated, auditable processes. Keys are never stored in plaintext and rotation is triggered on schedule or on-demand without service disruption.

Hybrid Classical-PQC Transition

During the migration period, hybrid schemes run classical and post-quantum algorithms in parallel, providing quantum resistance without breaking existing interoperability — a practical bridge while library and ecosystem support matures across the federal technology stack.

Cryptographic Dependency Mapping

Before migration can begin, programs need a clear inventory of where classical cryptography is used. We identify RSA, ECC, and Diffie-Hellman dependencies across software, libraries, TLS configurations, and certificate infrastructure.

Zero-Trust Integration

Cryptographic controls are integrated with our zero-trust network architecture — encrypted service-to-service communication, certificate-based identity, and continuous verification — so security is layered and not dependent on any single control.
